ViT has also been applied in several previous studies to distinguish between real images and fake or AI images. The researchers in [10] applied ViT-Base/16 and EfficientNet-B0 in their experiments using a dataset of 60,802 images
  including 30,401 real images from MSCOCO and 30,401 AI-generated images from SyntheticEye. The result is ViT obtained
  98% accuracy while EfficientNet-B0 only achieved 96%. ViT can capture global patterns better in determining
  AI-generated images, while EfficientNet-B0 is more efficient in training compared to ViT, so there is a trade-off
  between the two models. Another study presented by Lamichhane also stated that ViT with 16x16 patch size and data
  augmentation can outperform CNN architecture for AI image detection [12]. However, these studies only evaluated the
  models separately and not yet fusing CNN and ViT into one unified architecture. So the question about how to combine
  EfficientNet's local features with ViT's global attention is still an open question, and this becomes the motivation
  for the proposed fusion strategy that combines EfficientNet-B3 and ViT through an adaptive gating mechanism.
